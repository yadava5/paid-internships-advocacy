Worse Than No Internship At All

In NACE's Class of 2015 survey, graduates who had done an unpaid internship were offered a median of $34,375 — $4,197 less than classmates who had done no internship at all ($38,572). Paid interns were offered $53,521. On this measure an unpaid internship did not merely pay less than a paid one. It polled below doing nothing.

Who Goes Unpaid?

of Black interns in the Class of 2023 worked unpaid — the highest share of any group NACE reports, against an average of 40.8% across all interns. The burden is not carried evenly: 45.1% of Hispanic interns and 41.9% of White interns went unpaid, against 25.8% of Asian interns. 48.5% of women went unpaid, against 23.6% of men. Pell Grant recipients (46.5%) and first-generation students (46.2%) both sit above the average. Each bar is the share within that group — race is race and income is income, and this chart does not swap one for the other.

19 pts

The Number Nobody Agrees On

Ask two credible surveys about the same year and you get two different answers. NACE puts the unpaid share of the Class of 2023 at 40.8%. The National Survey of College Internships puts it at 31% at four-year institutions and 50% at two-year ones — ten points below NACE and nine above it, and nineteen points apart from each other. That spread is a real limit on how precisely anyone can state the unpaid share. A single decimal figure is one instrument's answer, not a settled fact.

40.8%

Progress, Not Victory

Unpaid internships are getting rarer. 53.5% of internships were unpaid in NACE's Class of 2014 survey; by the Class of 2023 it was 40.8% — a fall of nearly thirteen points in nine years. Two snapshots, not a continuous series: the line between them is dashed because nobody measured the years in between on the same basis. Advocacy moved this. Two in five students still work for free.
